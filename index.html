    Restoring independence in every step. A hands-free wearable that turns the space around you into touch.

    • The problem

    Mobility hazards are everywhere.

    Stairs, curbs, crowds, low light and head-level obstacles mean more falls and less independence.

    2.2Bpeople live with visual impairment worldwide
    46.7%of older adults with severe vision loss report falls, vs. 27.7% without

    Existing tools aren’t enough.

    • White caneArm’s reach and ground level only.
    • Guide dogCostly, with years of training.
    • Camera appsAudio only, one direction at a time.

    Stats: WHO, CDC.

    sensed (up to 4 m)warning (1.5 m)stop (0.5 m)

    • The solution

    Hands-free, all the way around.

    A haptic vest and a glasses clip. Something close on your left, you feel your left.

    Spoken cue · glasses clip

    “Stairs ahead, descending. First step in 2.10 meters.”

    • Glasses clip

    How the clip sees.

    Drag the chair anywhere in the hall. Switch layers to see each step.

    1. Detection boxesA box, a label and a confidence score.
    2. Nearest point to a cuePicks which side buzzes, and how hard.
    3. SegmentationFloor, wall, door, person, chair and sign.
    4. Depth from one cameraEvery pixel gets a distance. Blue is close, red is far.

    • Customer discovery

    What flipped.

    Comfort over code

    Vest comfort, cable routing and clear haptics came from wear tests, not the lab.

    Burned before

    OrCam’s $2–3K device was discontinued. Users want tech that stays supported.

    Learned in minutes

    Testers read the vibration cues within minutes.

    Value more

    Price has to cover training, warranty and service.

    • Competitive landscape

    An added layer, not a replacement.

    Locus works alongside a cane or guide dog. Battery life and upkeep are the gaps we’re closing next: about 4–6 hours today, a full day in Phase 1.

    • Business model
    • B2B first

    Start with institutions.

    Rehab centers, schools for the blind and state assistive-tech programs buy, train and lend. The same hardware works as a daily aid and an orientation-and-mobility training tool.
